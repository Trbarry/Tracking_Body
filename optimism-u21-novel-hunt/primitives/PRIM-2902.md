# PRIM-2902 — A withdrawal can make one portal originate an L2 deposit through a sibling portal

**PRIM-ID:** PRIM-2902

**Source contracts:** v9 `OptimismPortal2.sol`, `AddressAliasHelper.sol`.

**Exact functions:** `OptimismPortal2._isUnsafeTarget`,
`finalizeWithdrawalTransactionExternalProof`, `depositTransaction`.

**Trigger:** A valid withdrawal on chain A names portal B as its L1 target and encodes
`portalB.depositTransaction(to, value, gasLimit, false, data)`. Portal A calls portal B as
`msg.sender` during finalization.

**Attacker-controlled inputs:** The withdrawal controls portal B's L2 `_to`, `_value`, gas, and
calldata. Its L1 `msg.value` is the withdrawal value. A zero-value withdrawal can therefore emit a
deposit with zero mint but arbitrary nonzero L2 call value.

**Required state:** A valid chain-A withdrawal and a sibling portal not equal to portal A or portal
A's current lockbox. No shared-lockbox migration is required; the primitive exists today.

**State mutation:** Portal B meters the deposit and emits `TransactionDeposited`. If L1 value is
nonzero, portal B locks exactly `msg.value`. On L2 B, the derived transaction's sender is the
L1-to-L2 alias of portal A, its mint is `msg.value`, and its call value is the independent `_value`.

**External calls:** Portal A's withdrawal target call into portal B; portal B calls its current
lockbox only when `msg.value > 0`.

**Cross-contract dependency:** The target-chain derivation pipeline treats the portal-B event as
an L2 deposit. Any balance or authority assigned to portal A's alias on chain B becomes reachable
through attacker-chosen deposit calldata.

**Broken or weakened assumption:** The bad-target list is instance-local: it blocks only
`address(this)` and the current `ethLockbox`, not sibling portals. A portal address can consequently
be used as the apparent L1 origin of a transaction on another L2.

**Candidate compositions:** Drain a pre-existing alias balance by using `_value > msg.value`; call
an L2 contract that trusts the cross-portal alias; combine with a future portal/owner address that
is privileged on the sibling L2.

**Evidence:**

- `src/L1/OptimismPortal2.sol:845-849`: exact two-address unsafe-target list.
- `src/L1/OptimismPortal2.sol:617-630`: portal makes the target call with withdrawal data/value.
- `src/L1/OptimismPortal2.sol:730-795`: deposit locks `msg.value`, independently encodes `_value`,
  and aliases contract callers.
- `src/vendor/AddressAliasHelper.sol:21-31`: alias calculation.
- Deployed-bytecode fork test `test_realPortalBlocksOnlyItselfAndItsCurrentLockbox` reaches
  `Unproven`, not `BadTarget`, for a sibling portal.
- Fork test `test_siblingPortalAcceptsSourcePortalAndIndependentL2Value` verifies portal B emits a
  zero-mint, 7-ETH-call-value deposit sourced from portal A's alias.
- `mirror/lane-29/LIVE_STATE.md`: all 16 portal-alias/target-L2 combinations are currently zero
  balance, zero nonce, and no code.

**Confidence:** High for the primitive; high that no present core-contract impact was found.

**Reason it may be benign:** No v9 core L2 contract trusts any portal alias, every live
cross-portal alias is empty, and stealing funds stranded by somebody's direct contract deposit is
explicitly suppressed. The target's own portal cannot create its own alias context because it
blocks self-targeting. No candidate survived.
