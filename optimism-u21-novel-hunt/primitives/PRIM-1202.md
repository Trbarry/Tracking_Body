PRIM-ID: PRIM-1202 — a messenger withdrawal can still be finalized without being delivered: the path `OptimismPortal_L1CDMCallFailed` does not cover

Source contracts: src/L1/OptimismPortal2.sol, src/universal/CrossDomainMessenger.sol, src/L1/L1CrossDomainMessenger.sol (v9 @ ba6fcadb).

Exact functions: OptimismPortal2.finalizeWithdrawalTransactionExternalProof (L577-666; reentrancy guard L594, new revert
  L651-653); CrossDomainMessenger.relayMessage (L239-248 other-messenger branch, L265-282 "mark failed and return" when
  `hasMinGas` fails OR `xDomainMsgSender != DEFAULT_L2_SENDER`); L1CrossDomainMessenger._isOtherMessenger (L136-138).

Trigger: attacker replays one of his own failed messages directly on the L1CDM; the message target (his contract)
  calls `portal.finalizeWithdrawalTransactionExternalProof(victimTx, victimSubmitter)` for a matured messenger
  withdrawal of somebody else.

Attacker-controlled inputs: his own message (target, gas), the moment of the replay, which victim withdrawal.

Required state: (a) attacker owns a message in `failedMessages` (send one from L2 whose target reverts on the first
  relay); (b) victim's messenger withdrawal is proven and past the 7-day maturity (anyone may finalize it).

State mutation: `portal.finalizedWithdrawals[victim] = true`; `L1CDM.failedMessages[victimMsg] = true`;
  `successfulMessages[victimMsg]` stays false; the victim's ETH moves ETHLockbox -> L1CDM and waits there.

External calls: L1CDM.relayMessage(attacker msg) -> attacker target -> portal.finalize -> lockbox.unlockETH ->
  L1CDM.relayMessage(victim msg) [returns early] .

Cross-contract dependency: during a REPLAY the portal's `l2Sender` is the default value, so the portal's guard does
  not see that the messenger is mid-relay; the messenger's own guard is `xDomainMsgSender`, and its reaction is to
  record the message as failed and return success to the portal.

Broken or weakened assumption: the U21 notice describes the hardening as "the withdrawal stays retryable instead of
  being finalized as failed". That holds when the messenger REVERTS. When the messenger returns normally after
  parking the message, the withdrawal is finalized and delivery depends on a later replay. This is the pre-U21
  design and remains so.

Candidate compositions:
  - an L1 integration that treats `WithdrawalFinalized(hash, true)` as "message executed" (the event carries
    `success = true` here);
  - anything that depends on the ETH being in the lockbox rather than the messenger between finalize and replay;
  - combined with a target that can never succeed later (then it is the sender's own problem, as before).

Evidence: test/poc/lane12/Lane12.t.sol::test_R05_sibling_replayReentrancy_finalizesVictimAsFailedButReplayable —
  re-entered finalize does not revert; victim withdrawal finalized; successfulMessages false, failedMessages true;
  lockbox −5 ETH, L1CDM +5 ETH; then an unrelated account calls `relayMessage` and the receiver gets the 5 ETH with the
  correct `xDomainMessageSender`. Proof records are written with `vm.store` against the real anchor game (see the
  setup note in the test file); everything executed afterwards is the deployed bytecode.
  Sibling outcomes for comparison: test_R05_checkedPath_unsafeTarget_reverts_L1CDMCallFailed,
  test_R05_sibling_otherSender_failure_isFinalizedAsFailed, test_R05_checkKeysOnSenderOnly.

Confidence: high.

Reason it may be benign: it is. The message is replayable by anyone, the value is held by the messenger for exactly
  that message hash, and the attacker pays the gas. Same result as lane 04 PRIM-0401; listed here as the answer to
  "which sibling path lacks the new check".
