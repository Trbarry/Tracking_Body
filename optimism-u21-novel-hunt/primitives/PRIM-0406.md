PRIM-ID: PRIM-0406 - proof bookkeeping asymmetries (`deleteProvenWithdrawal`, `proofSubmitters`, prove-after-finalize)

Source contracts: OptimismPortal2 v6.1.0
Exact functions: `deleteProvenWithdrawal` (:711-728), `proveWithdrawalTransaction` (:390-492), `numProofSubmitters`
Trigger / facts:
  - delete is permissionless and only possible when the stored game is `CHALLENGER_WINS` or blacklisted in the
    CURRENT `anchorStateRegistry` (:719-720). It clears `provenWithdrawals[h][s]` only; `proofSubmitters[h]` keeps
    the address, and every re-prove pushes again (duplicates; array is append-only and unbounded).
  - prove never checks `finalizedWithdrawals`: proofs can be (re)written for an already finalized hash forever.
  - a submitter's re-prove overwrites his own entry and resets only his own timer; other submitters' mature
    proofs are untouched; any caller can finalize with any submitter's proof.
  - delete cannot touch a proof whose game is IN_PROGRESS or DEFENDER_WINS and not blacklisted, including the
    proof of an already finalized withdrawal.
Attacker-controlled inputs: hash, submitter (delete); full proof (prove).
Required state: for delete, Guardian blacklist or a lost game.
State mutation: mapping entry zeroed / array growth.
Broken or weakened assumption: "proofSubmitters lists live proofs" - it lists every historical prove call.
  "Both conditions are permanent" holds only for a fixed ASR pointer (`migrateToSharedDisputeGame` swaps it).
Candidate compositions: off-chain finalizers that iterate `proofSubmitters` can be made to walk attacker-inflated
  arrays (griefing of tooling, not of funds). Nothing on-chain iterates the array.
Evidence: `Real.t.sol::test_delete_paths` (Guardian prank = privileged SETUP of a blacklist),
  `::test_multi_submitters_single_execution`, `::test_reprove_resets_own_timer_only`,
  `::test_feevault_etched_reverting_target_thirdparty` (prove after finalize succeeds, checkWithdrawal reverts
  AlreadyFinalized). Live example: hash 0xc7b858d7…eeb1 already has 4 submitter entries.
Confidence: high.
Reason it may be benign: deletable proofs are exactly the ones `checkWithdrawal` already rejects
  (`isGameClaimValid` needs not-blacklisted and DEFENDER_WINS); replay protection is keyed by hash only.
