PRIM-ID: PRIM-2503
Source contracts: `OptimismPortal2`.
Exact functions: `proveWithdrawalTransaction`, `finalizeWithdrawalTransaction`, `finalizeWithdrawalTransactionExternalProof`, `checkWithdrawal`.
Trigger: An attacker proves another user's real withdrawal, including against an attacker-created respected game, while the honest submitter also has or later creates a proof.
Attacker-controlled inputs: Proof transaction caller, game/root (subject to game validity and bond), output/storage proofs, repetition.
Required state: A genuine withdrawal inclusion proof and a proper game that was respected at creation and has not yet lost.
State mutation: The attacker can write only `provenWithdrawals[hash][attacker]`; the honest submitter's cell is disjoint.
External calls: Finalization validates only the explicitly supplied proof-submitter cell.
Cross-contract dependency: DGF permits type-9 creation for the exact `0.08 ether` bond; game resolution may later invalidate the attacker's root.
Broken or weakened assumption: Anyone may prove anyone's withdrawal, but proof authority is namespaced by `msg.sender`, not by withdrawal sender or target.
Candidate compositions: Bad-proof blocking; overwrite; malicious first-array entry; timer-reset grief; attacker-controlled losing game.
Evidence: `OptimismPortal2.sol:452-487,496-498,577-607,671-705`; OP Mainnet N reads show respected type 9 and bond `80000000000000000`; fork test `test_badSubmitterProofDoesNotBlockGoodSubmitterProof` places the bad submitter first and confirms the good proof still validates before and after bad-proof deletion.
Confidence: High.
Reason it may be benign: There is no automatic proof selection and no cross-address overwrite. An attacker cannot write or reset the victim's mapping cell without being the victim address.
