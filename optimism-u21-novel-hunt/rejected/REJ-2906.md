# REJ-2906 — Existing unbacked or omitted ETH lets one chain consume the other's backing

**Idea.** OP Mainnet or Unichain enters the shared set with circulating L2 ETH not represented in
its migrated portal/lockbox balance, then valid withdrawals socialize that deficit through the
common pool.

**Status:** Not promoted; no concrete production-reachable deficit source found.

**Exact killing/limiting facts:**

- At block 26433900 both planned portals have zero balance and their old lockboxes hold
  182541.068255115703234018 ETH (OP) and 18266.157945407766718622 ETH (Unichain). The migrator
  sweeps both portal balances and entire lockbox balances (`OPContractsManagerMigrator.sol:515-534`).
- The L1StandardBridge and L1CrossDomainMessenger balances for all four chains are zero. DelayedWETH
  balances are dispute bonds with separate ownership/withdrawal accounting, not L2 native-ETH
  backing.
- Deposits lock exactly `msg.value` before emitting the L2 mint field; withdrawals originate from a
  message-passer entry and release exactly `_tx.value` (`OptimismPortal2.sol:612-627,730-795`).
- The interop `ETHLiquidity` predeploy is currently uninitialized and zero balance on all four L2s.
  When interop activates, v9 restricts `mint`/`burn` to the SuperchainETHBridge
  (`L2/ETHLiquidity.sol:37-48`); its intentionally preloaded reserve is excluded from circulating
  backing and source burn/destination mint conserve aggregate circulating ETH.
- No transaction can deposit during the single atomic L1 migration window.

Limitation: standard JSON-RPC does not expose an enumerable total of all L2 account balances, so
this is not a proof that historical circulating supply equals L1 custody to the wei. It is a
negative result: no non-fault mint, omitted custody account, or permissionless conversion of the
ETHLiquidity reserve into an L1 withdrawal was identified. A separate supply-accounting lane could
independently reconcile historical deposit/withdrawal and state-migration data.
