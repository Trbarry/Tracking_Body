# REJ-2305 — Missing bad targets expose lockbox/system contracts during callback

## Hypothesis

The portal blocks only itself and its current lockbox. A withdrawal might target an
old lockbox, SystemConfig, ASR, ProxyAdmin, or messenger and use the portal caller
identity plus unlocked ETH to drain or duplicate custody.

## Killing fact

The current portal and lockbox revert with `OptimismPortal_BadTarget` before proof
lookup (`OptimismPortal2.sol:598-607,845-849`). Privileged configuration/migration
methods reject the portal caller (`ProxyAdminOwnedBase.sol:88-105`). An old lockbox
may still authorize the portal, but during the callback its `unlockETH` rejects
because portal `l2Sender != 0xdead` (`ETHLockbox.sol:157-174`); `lockETH` can only
put the withdrawal's own value into the obsolete box, a self-harm path. Calling the
messenger's payable outbound path deposits the same value back through the portal,
creating matching new backing/liability, while fresh `relayMessage` is authenticated.

## Evidence

`test_badTargetsRejectedBeforeProofLookup` and
`test_lockboxRejectsUnauthorizedAndInWithdrawalUnlocks`; manual function-by-function
review of the named targets. No method callable as the portal spends lockbox value a
second time.

## Conclusion

Killed for current v9. Persistent old-lockbox authorization remains a useful future
composition, but today it yields no permissionless profit or third-party impact.
