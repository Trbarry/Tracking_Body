# REJ-2903 — Poison or race the planned migration with deployments or ETH donations

**Idea.** A permissionless actor predeploys the future shared proxies, donates to a future/old
lockbox or portal, or deposits during the per-chain loop so the migration silently omits or
misaccounts liquidity.

**Status:** Rejected.

**Exact killing facts:**

- The shared proxies are deployed with CREATE2 from the delegatecall context
  (`Blueprint.sol:124-133`; `OPContractsManagerMigrator.sol:168-222`). In the production action that
  context is the owner Safe. An attacker calling/delegatecalling OPCM uses a different deployer
  address and cannot occupy the Safe's CREATE2 address.
- The public migrator/OPCM entry points do not by themselves grant authority. The first proxy
  upgrade and every chain mutation call the real ProxyAdmin/SystemConfig/portal/lockbox, whose
  owner checks require the common Safe. Calls from an attacker contract revert and atomically roll
  back any attacker-context deployments (`OPContractsManagerUtils.sol:312-386` and
  `ProxyAdminOwnedBase.sol:88-105`).
- The migration is one Ethereum transaction. No unrelated deposit can interleave between chains.
  The only ETH-bearing calls are portal -> known new lockbox and old lockbox -> known new lockbox;
  the receiving functions make no attacker callback (`ETHLockbox.sol:132-151,198-216`).
- Pre-migration ETH in a portal is swept at `OPContractsManagerMigrator.sol:515-524`; the entire old
  lockbox balance is swept at lines 526-534. A donation to the not-yet-deployed CREATE2 address
  survives deployment as extra collateral. There is no balance-equality condition to corrupt.
- Validator checks require post-migration portal and retired-lockbox balances to be zero
  (`OPContractsManagerMigrationValidator.sol:536-577`).

Donations after migration can make a retired lockbox nonzero, but only add inaccessible surplus and
do not create L2 liability or permissionless loss.
